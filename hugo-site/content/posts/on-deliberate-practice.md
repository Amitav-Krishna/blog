One thing I have pondered of late is what I wish to deliberately practice.  These thoughts have been stoked by many pieces of writing, but one that has been especially great is [Tyler Cowen's](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/07/how-i-practice-at-what-i-do.html) piece on this topic.  

One great quote Cowen mentions is the following:
> What is it you do to train that is comparable to a pianist practicing scales?  

Sadly, I don't have a great response to this!  The area in which this is most applicable is working out.  Recently I have begun weight-lifting, and while it's not a very exciting response, that is quite comparable to a pianist's scales.  When it comes to my work however, I have never found intentional repetition to be an especially effective method to improve my abilities.  The greatest difficulty I face in this sort of deliberate practice is that currently, my work is very varied.  It's a mixture of engineering, machine learning, physics, and more recently chemistry.  One thing I tried to do last October was commit to reading a different machine learning every week or so, but I found that ineffective at growing my skills.  The only effective method I have found as of yet to improving my skills is to simply build projects.  

There are areas, however, that I wish to excel in that are not my work.  One of these is reading comprehension.  A habit I've tried to pick up is reading everyday, but I've found it difficult to force myself to stick with this.  This is mostly with non-fiction, and with fiction I can usually complete it faster, but non-fiction is the category in which I most wish to build my comprehension skills.

A final skill I've tried to build is my photography.  My approach to this is to take photos, of a consistent subject (geese), almost-daily.  I used to try to make a habit of, from these photos, curating and describing and editing them, and making a blog post / X thread out of them, but I found that this put me off of the photography, because the accessory work took a much greater amount of time (often two or three times) the amount of time required to do the actual photography.

That's all from me!
