One spoiler (which shouldn't be that surprising) is that there exists an artificial, extremely powerful, superintelligent entity in the book which I will refer to as John.
I think overall it provided an interesting perspective on one potential failure mode of a system like John, which in the book is lack of meaning.  I know this is a pretty common concern, but I think the book does a great job of making it very visceral.  In particular, it paints a very good picture of how Asimov's Laws of robotics can go wrong:
> Asimov's Laws of robotics, from "Runaround"
1. Protect human safety,
2. Follow human orders, unless the conflict with the first law,
3. Protect the self, unless it conflicts with the first and second law.
Because there's been like three spoiler warnings, the specific failure mode it gives is that after coming into power, many humans begin taking on forms that are not usually associated with humans, such as becoming animals or zombies or even just becoming incorporeal.  This has a bunch of consequences in the book, but I think this is a really good point: if a human order can make a human not human, thereby exempting the human from the first law, that would be putting it in danger of dying from the robot, therefore a human can never become *not* a human.  Is this actually true?  Maybe not.  
Another interesting specific detail from the book was that many humans just isolated themselves completely from other humans, because John provided everything for them and could even create "fake" humans for them to interact with.
